## 2. Formats, lengths, hooks and visual styles that perform

### Takeaway
The consistent advice for educational Shorts is a hook in the first 1–2 seconds, an aim of 70% or more of viewers choosing "viewed" over "swiped away," and a length of about 30–45 seconds for explainers. Explainers need slightly more time than the 15–30 seconds that suits entertainment. Named formats that work include myth-busters, "did you know" clips and step-by-step breakdowns with on-screen numbers and captions. All of this evidence comes from vendor blogs, not platform data.

### Cited Findings
- On length, OpusClip's blog says educational content "can sustain slightly longer formats (35-45 seconds) if the payoff is clear," while entertainment does best at 18–25 seconds. Shorts of 15–30 seconds "consistently achieve the highest retention rates, often exceeding 80%." — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- The search summary attributed to OpusClip the claim that "45-second versions generated 3x more subscribers per view compared to shorter alternatives for educational finance content." This is a single-source vendor claim with unknown sample size; I could not verify it. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- Hook and swipe benchmarks:
  - Aim for a 70–90% "viewed" rate on YouTube's Viewed vs. Swiped Away metric. Distribution reportedly "collapse[s]" below 60%.
  - "Over 70% of the viewers who leave do it within the first 2 seconds."
  - "Shorts with an immediate hook in the first 2 seconds retain 19% more viewers."
  - These come from third-party blogs such as Shortimize, prepublish.ai and ReelRise, with methods not stated. — [prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts); [ReelRise](https://reelrise.app/guide/viewed-vs-swiped-away-the-only-youtube-shorts-metric-that-matters/); [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-does-youtube-shorts-algorithm-work)
- On retention targets, an average percentage viewed of 70% or more is "healthy" for Shorts, and below 50% signals a hook or pacing problem. — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-benchmarks)
- Formats for finance explainers:
  - "Explainers on topics people are actively searching for tend to perform best."
  - "Myth-busters, 'did you know' formats, and step-by-step breakdowns also drive strong engagement."
  - An example 45-second myth-buster template: state the myth, bust it with a specific fact, end with one action step.
  - Put charts and figures on screen at the moment they are mentioned.
  - These come from Captions.ai, a vendor. — [Captions.ai](https://captions.ai/solutions/financial-advisors-and-creators)
- Tax creators on short-form lean on tax myths, audit flags and IRS updates, delivered with humor. — [Verito](https://verito.com/blog/top-accounting-influencers/)
- OpusClip also publishes "hook formulas that drive 3-second holds" (content not read). — [OpusClip hooks](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas)

### Inferences
- For a voice-over-graphics accounting channel, 30–45 seconds, with the thesis or myth stated on screen in the first second, fits the vendor consensus.
- Recurring series ("term of the day," myth vs. fact) are template-like by design. See section 4 on the "generic or repetitive" risk. Each episode needs distinct commentary and substance so it does not read as interchangeable.

### Gaps
- No platform-published (YouTube or TikTok) or independent dataset comparing formats (myth vs. fact, listicle, stitch or duet, whiteboard, series) for finance or accounting specifically.
- Stitch and duet performance data for finance: none found.
- The length ceiling for Shorts. My understanding is that YouTube raised it to 3 minutes in October 2024, but I did not re-verify this in this session. Some benchmark blogs still say "60 seconds or shorter."

## 3. Faceless / voice-over vs on-camera: performance and trust

### Takeaway
The one sizable dataset found (7,431 videos) says on-camera content accounts for most viral hits. It also finds the face effect strongest for small creators and at the start of the video. That supports a hybrid: the owner's face or photo at the open, then voice over graphics. Evidence specific to trust in finance topics is weak, apart from YouTube's policy against AI "expert" personas.

### Cited Findings
- A Content Labs data study analyzed 7,431 short-form videos across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube. It found that face-on-camera content "owns 1,345 of the 1,598 viral hits over 100K views (84%)." This is a vendor study, and the method and date were not visible. The page itself was blocked. — [The Content Labs](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/faceless-vs-face-on-camera-data-study)
- The same study, per the search summary, cites a February 2025 paper in the *International Journal of Research in Marketing*. The paper found "moderate face presence at the beginning of videos facilitates optimal engagement, not maximum face time throughout." It also found the face effect "is less pronounced for influencers with higher follower counts," which the study calls a "trust bootstrap" for small creators. I did not locate or check the paper itself. — [The Content Labs](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/faceless-vs-face-on-camera-data-study)
- On the faceless side, the claimed advantage is output: batching "7 Shorts in the time a traditional creator shoots one." This is from vendors selling faceless tools. — [virvid.ai](https://virvid.ai/blog/faceless-shorts-dominance-strategy-2026); [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/faceless-youtube-channel-ideas-for-2025/)
- Rowan Cheung is cited as growing an AI-news TikTok "without ever filming, talking, or showing up on camera." This is anecdotal. — [TikTok @rowancheung](https://www.tiktok.com/@rowancheung)

### Inferences
- The owner's photo on the first frame plus his own voice over graphics lines up with the "face at the beginning" finding. It is weaker than live on-camera footage, but a real, named human voice is a trust signal. It also helps separate the channel from the "AI persona" pattern in YouTube's July 2026 clarification (section 4).
- For finance and tax, where credibility matters, showing credentials (CPA or experience) on screen is likely more important than face time. This is an inference; no study was found on it.

### Gaps
- No independent study on trust in faceless versus on-camera formats for finance or accounting topics specifically.
- Whether a static photo on frame one produces the same effect as live face presence has not been studied, as far as I found.

## 4. Policy: YouTube reused/inauthentic content (2025–2026), AI disclosure, TikTok AIGC labels

### Takeaway
There are two dated policy events. The first came on July 15, 2025, when YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content," covering mass-produced and templated content. The second came around July 13–16, 2026, when YouTube split inauthentic content into three buckets:
- generic or repetitive content
- unsatisfying or off-putting content
- AI personas that present as human experts giving finance, legal, health or political advice

The reused-content policy (commentary, clips, reactions) was unchanged. A real person narrating his own analysis over original graphics is monetizable in principle. The risk is videos that look templated and interchangeable, and Shorts that repackage other creators' videos without transformative commentary.

### Cited Findings
- **July 15, 2025 update:** YouTube made "a minor update" to its "repetitious content" policy "to better clarify that it includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced," and renamed it "inauthentic content." YouTube said such content "has always been ineligible" and that there was "no change to YouTube's reused content policy," which covers commentary, clips, compilations and reactions. — [YouTube Help (via search summary)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [AlternativeTo, Jul 2025](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content)
- **Inauthentic content wording** (as quoted in snippets):
  - Content that "follows a template with minimal variation," is "easy to reproduce at scale," and lacks clear author input or meaningful substance.
  - Examples: "channels that upload narrative stories with only superficial differences between them," "slideshows that all have the same narration," and "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value."
  - [YouTube Help (via search summary)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [Sybrid](https://sybrid.com/resources/blog/youtube-monetization-updates-2025/); [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025)
- **July 2026 clarification:**
  - Tubefilter (July 13, 2026) and TechCrunch (July 20, 2026) reported that YouTube reorganized inauthentic content into three categories:
    - "Generic or repetitive content": template-looking or interchangeable when several are watched in a row.
    - "Unsatisfying or off-putting content": content that "relies heavily on emotionally manipulative formulas" or "appears designed to shock or surprise viewers for the sole purpose of getting views."
    - AI personas: "any content that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, finances, or politics."
  - Both outlets say the rules themselves did not change; this was a clarification.
  - One snippet gives July 16 as the rollout date.
  - [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026)
- A secondary claim says enforcement moved to "channel-level detection" rather than video-by-video. It is unverified and comes from a vendor or aggregator. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)
- **Reused content:**
  - Allowed if "viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video."
  - Reused content is defined as repurposing existing content "without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications or educational or entertainment value."
  - The allowed example is "Reaction videos where you comment on the original video."
  - Not allowed: compilations or clips "with little or no narrative," "short videos compiled from other social media."
  - These rules apply "regardless of whether you have permission."
  - [YouTube Help (via search summary)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/)
- **YouTube altered or synthetic disclosure** (introduced March 2024, per my background knowledge):
  - Creators must disclose "realistic content – content a viewer could easily mistake for a real person, place, scene, or event" made with altered or synthetic media, including generative AI.
  - Examples: using a realistic person's likeness or replacing their face, altering footage of real events, and generating realistic scenes.
  - No disclosure is needed for content that is "clearly unrealistic, animated, includes special effects," or for AI used for productivity ("scripts, content ideas, or automatic captions").
  - The disclosure is a toggle in upload details.
  - Persistent non-disclosure can lead to content removal or YPP suspension.
  - [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/); [YouTube Help: Disclosing use of GenAI](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)
- **TikTok:**
  - Labeling is required for AI-generated content depicting realistic people or scenes. It is a disclosure rule, not a ban.
  - TikTok adopted C2PA Content Credentials (May 9, 2024) and auto-labels uploads carrying that metadata.
  - It is adding "invisible watermarking" to AIGC made with TikTok tools such as AI Editor Pro.
  - It reports having labeled "over 1.3 billion" AI-generated videos.
  - In November 2025 it added a user setting that controls how much AI-generated content appears in the feed.
  - [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en); [TechCrunch, Nov 18 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/); [NBC News, 2024](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/tiktok-will-automatically-label-ai-generated-content-rcna151446)

### Inferences (applied to a channel with the owner's photo on frame one and his own voice over graphics)
- **Own voice and own analysis over original graphics.** The policy text targets content with "minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" and "slideshows that all have the same narration." Original narrated explanation by a real, named person is educational commentary, so it is not the prohibited pattern as written. The practical risk is a series whose episodes look interchangeable: same template, same structure, only the nouns swapped. That matches the July 2026 "generic or repetitive" description.
- **Summarizing other creators' long-form videos** falls under the reused-content test. Clipping or re-cutting their footage needs substantive transformation (commentary, critique, added analysis). A voiced summary using original graphics and no borrowed footage is safer under reused content. It still needs real added value, and copyright or fair use is a separate question not researched here.
- **The "AI expert persona" clause is aimed at synthetic hosts** giving finance advice. A real accountant's own voice and photo is the opposite case. If he ever uses an AI voice clone or AI avatar of himself, that likely does trigger the altered or synthetic disclosure (realistic likeness or voice), and the persona clause would deserve a close reading.
- **Disclosure for AI-generated graphics.** Stylized or animated charts would not need YouTube's disclosure under the "clearly unrealistic/animated" and "production assistance" exemptions. Photorealistic AI scenes or people would.

### Gaps
- The verbatim current text of YouTube Help 1311392 and 14328491, and the official July 2026 YouTube announcement. All were blocked; the wording above is quoted in snippets and not verified.
- Whether YouTube's policy explicitly addresses synthetic voice (voice cloning) of the creator's own voice. Not confirmed.
- TikTok's exact Community Guidelines wording on the AIGC label. Only secondary snippets were available.

## 5. Published benchmarks for Shorts in education/finance

### Takeaway
Only vendor-blog benchmarks are available, with no education- or finance-specific platform data:
- Healthy Shorts show 70% or more average percentage viewed.
- The typical view range scales with channel size (for example, 50–500 views per Short at under 1K subscribers).
- Roughly 1.2–1.8 subscribers per 1,000 Short views.

Treat all of these as directional.

### Cited Findings
- **Views by channel size** (first 48 hours): under 1K subscribers, 50–500 views per Short; 1K–10K, 200–2,000; 10K–100K, 1,000–10,000. — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks)
- **Subscriber conversion:** a Short with 10,000+ views brings "12-18 new subscribers," which is about 1.2–1.8 per 1,000 views. — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks)
- **Retention:**
  - Average Shorts retention is 73%, versus 52% for long-form. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate)
  - 60–70% of viewers watch to the end. — [Zebracat](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/youtube-shorts-statistics)
  - Educational and how-to niches reach "42%+ average APV." This conflicts with the 70% "healthy" figure, and the source's definition is unclear. — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-benchmarks); [socialrails](https://socialrails.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-complete-guide)
- **Engagement:** "average engagement rate of 5.91%" for YouTube Shorts, said to lead TikTok and Reels. This is from an aggregator and the original source is unknown. — [Loopex Digital](https://www.loopexdigital.com/blog/youtube-shorts-statistics)
- **Ad spend:** advertisers reportedly spent 20–25% more on Shorts ads in 2025 than in 2024. — [Zebracat](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/youtube-shorts-statistics)
- Dash Social publishes 2026 YouTube benchmarks (not read). — [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/youtube)

### Inferences
- A reasonable internal target for a new accounting Shorts channel: a viewed rate of 70% or more, APV of 70% or more on 30–45 second Shorts, and about 1–2 subscribers per 1,000 views. Expect a few hundred views per Short until the channel has about 1K subscribers.

### Gaps
- No Tubular, YouTube or TikTok official benchmarks for education or finance Shorts were accessible.
- No RPM or Shorts revenue data specific to finance was found in this pass.
